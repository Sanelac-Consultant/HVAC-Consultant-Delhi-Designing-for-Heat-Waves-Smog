Delhi hands building designers two opposite problems inside a single year. May and June push afternoon temperatures into the mid-forties, and by November the air itself turns hostile, with GRAP restrictions tightening as the AQI climbs into the severe band. A system sized only for cooling tonnage cannot survive both. ### Heat Waves Are Rewriting the Cooling Load Sheet

Delhi’s peak afternoons used to sit comfortably inside the design conditions printed in older reference tables. Today the mid-forties arrive early in May and stay for weeks, and a few days overshoot even that. Air-cooled chillers, VRF outdoor units, and rooftop packaged units all lose capacity as ambient temperature climbs, and the loss is steepest exactly when the building needs the most cooling. A machine rated at 35°C ambient may deliver noticeably less at 46°C, and a layout that crowds condensers onto an enclosed terrace makes the recirculation penalty worse.

A seasoned [**HVAC Consultant Delhi**](https://sanelac.com/service/hvac-consultant/) therefore asks for the ambient design basis before touching a tonnage figure. We derate equipment at the actual site temperature, check condenser clearances against hot-air short-circuiting, and push the architect early on glazing ratios, shading, and roof insulation, because every watt of solar gain stopped at the envelope is a watt the plant never has to remove. Staged capacity, variable-speed compressors, and N+1 redundancy on critical floors cover the extremes without punishing the other ten months.

### Smog Season Turns Filtration into a Core Design Item

From late October, Delhi’s air moves through the Poor, Very Poor, and Severe bands, and GRAP stages tighten as the AQI rises. Indoors, that means PM2.5 and PM10 loads that standard office filtration was never built for. When a client reaches out to an [**HVAC Consultant Delhi**](https://sanelac.com/service/hvac-consultant/) in smog season, the conversation has shifted from chiller brands to filter classes, pressure drops, and how clean the air really is after it passes the coil.

A workable filtration train for a commercial building in the capital usually runs in stages:

- A coarse pre-filter at the intake louver and AHU inlet to catch dust and protect everything downstream.
- A fine filter in the F7 to F9 range, or MERV 13 equivalent, to deal with PM2.5.
- Final-stage HEPA or activated carbon in critical zones, data areas, and premium offices that need gaseous pollutant control.

Filtration is not a free upgrade. Finer media raises resistance, loaded filters raise it further, and fans selected on clean-filter pressure will starve the floor by January. We size fans for mid-to-final resistance, specify differential pressure gauges across every bank, keep AHU casings and ductwork tightly sealed so unfiltered air does not slip around the media, and leave enough service clearance that maintenance teams can actually change filters during a smog week.

### Fresh Air Is the Hardest Balancing Act

Ventilation standards still demand outdoor air for occupants, and in Delhi that air is a double burden. In summer, it carries a heavy sensible and latent load that can account for a quarter to a third of the cooling demand in a densely occupied office. In winter, smog carries particulates that the filters must then remove.

Treating outdoor air through a dedicated outdoor air system with energy recovery changes the equation. A heat or enthalpy wheel, or a plate exchanger, uses exhaust air to pre-cool incoming air in May and recovers a share of that conditioning, so the main cooling plant sees a far smaller fresh-air load. Pair it with CO2-based demand control, and the building admits what its occupants need instead of a fixed worst-case quantity. On every project, an [**HVAC Consultant Delhi**](https://sanelac.com/service/hvac-consultant/) owner can rely on treating fresh-air quantity as a calculated figure tied to the design standard and real occupancy, not a round number copied from the last job.

### Controls and IBMS Hold the Design Together

Good equipment still fails without intelligence behind it. Four decades across MEP and IBMS work have shown me that most smog-season complaints trace back to control logic nobody revisited after commissioning. An IBMS that reads an outdoor air quality sensor can switch the building into a smog mode: fresh air drops to the minimum the CO2 reading allows, recirculation through the fine filters rises, and facility managers get an alert before a filter bank reaches its final pressure. In a heat wave, the same system stages chillers, resets chilled water temperature, and pre-cools floors early in the morning, before the afternoon peak hits the plant.

### FAQs

### Q1. Does smog season call for different HVAC filtration?

Yes. Smog-season PM2.5 levels overwhelm basic filters, so commercial buildings need staged filtration with a fine filter in the F7 to F9 range, sealed AHU casings, and pressure monitoring. Critical areas may add HEPA or carbon stages. Fan selection must allow for the higher resistance that loaded filters create.

### Q2. Should fresh-air intake be cut during severe smog days?

Reduce it, but only to the code minimum that CO2 readings support. Closing intakes completely lets carbon dioxide build up and affects occupant alertness. A demand-controlled system with an outdoor air quality feed can lower intake automatically while the fine filters keep recirculated air clean.

### Q3. Can chillers lose capacity during Delhi heat waves?

Yes. Air-cooled chillers and VRF outdoor units deliver less cooling as ambient temperature rises, often by a noticeable margin at mid-forties conditions. Selecting equipment against the site’s real design temperature, allowing proper condenser clearances, and staging capacity keeps the building comfortable at the peak.

### Q4. Is energy recovery worth including in Delhi projects?

Usually, yes. Delhi’s fresh-air load is heavy in both summer and winter, and an enthalpy wheel or plate exchanger recovers part of that energy from exhaust air. Payback depends on occupancy, operating hours, and fan penalties, so it should be checked against each project’s actual load profile.
